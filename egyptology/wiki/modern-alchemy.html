I. The Ontology of Modern Practice — Systematic Re-activation

Modern Alchemy is not a nostalgic revival of a dead pseudo-science, but the Systematic Re-activation of the Unbroken Chain connecting Ancient Kemet to the present. The work of the House of Life continues through dedicated circles of practitioners who utilize traditional laboratory methods alongside modern consciousness studies.

In this research framework, modern alchemy is viewed as High-Fidelity Frequency Management. The practitioner seeks to refine their own Composite Soul (Ba, Ka, Ib) while simultaneously working with material substances to manifest higher-dimensional data into the physical plane. Alchemy is the Science of Transmutation—the technology used to bridge the gap between the "Lead" of habitual, low-frequency existence and the "Gold" of transfigured, high-frequency awareness (Akh).

II. Defining the Art — The Raising of Vibrations

Within the modern tradition's own vocabulary, to practise alchemy is to raise the vibration of a substance — and, by correspondence, of the practitioner. The phrase is the tradition's self-description, recorded here as such: a prepared medicine is said to carry a finer, more coherent pattern than the crude material it was made from, and the operator's task across the operations of §III is to refine matter and awareness together, each serving as the measure of the other.

The term belongs to the modern revival rather than to the older literature, whose authors spoke instead of purification, exaltation, and the separation of the subtle from the gross. It is not a quantity in physics, and this archive does not present it as one; it names how contemporary practitioners understand what they are doing, in the same way that Ma'at alignment (§XI) names the ethical condition they hold the work to require.

III. The Wet Path — Laboratory Operativity

The Wet Path (Via Humida) operates through the physical laboratory. It utilizes glassware, heat, and solvents (menstrua) to perform operations on plants, minerals, and metals.

In the modern context, the laboratory acts as a Microcosmic Map of the practitioner’s internal process. Every distillation, calcination, and conjunction performed in the vessel is a physical demonstration of the practitioner’s ability to direct Heka. The Wet Path provides an Objective Feedback Loop: the physical changes in the substance (the color stages of Black, White, and Red) are indicators of the successfully applied theurgical intention. See Alchemy — The Sacred Art.

IV. The Dry Path — Internalized Theurgy

Alongside the Wet Path of §III, the literature of the Work distinguishes a Dry Path (Via Secca, also called the short or royal way): work carried out in the crucible at high temperature, without the prolonged solvent circulations of the humid way, and correspondingly faster and harder to control. In the accounts transmitted by Fulcanelli and set out most openly by his pupil Eugène Canseliet, the dry way is bound up with the antimony work — stibnite and its regulus — and is treated as the more dangerous of the two routes precisely because its fires are literal and its stages compressed.

The contrast the texts draw is one of tempo and risk rather than of goal: the Wet Path works over months at gentle heats in glass, the Dry Path in days at furnace heat in the crucible, and both are presented as roads to the same Stone. This archive records the distinction as the literature states it; the internalised reading of the Dry Path as work within the practitioner's own vessel is taken up at §XVI.

V. Plant Spagyrics — The Extraction of Essences

Spagyric medicine is the most accessible point of entry for modern laboratory work. It involves the separation and recombination of a plant's three principles:

SULFUR (SOUL)

The essential oils and active volatile principles. Represents the plant's unique Vibrational Signature. Corresponds to the Ba.

MERCURY (SPIRIT)

The alcohol produced through fermentation. Represents the Carrier Wave of the life-force. Corresponds to the Ka.

SALT (BODY)

The mineral salts extracted from the plant's calcined ash. Represents the Grounding Matrix. Corresponds to the Khat.

Recombining these purified principles creates an Integrated Medicine. Unlike standard herbal extracts, a spagyric preparation addresses the patient's entire composite being, restoring balance through "Vibrational Realignment."

VI. Mineral Alchemy — The Purified Earth

Beyond the plant work of §V, the modern curriculum takes up the mineral kingdom: salts, and then the metals and their ores, worked through the acetate route and kindred preparations transmitted in the Paracelsian literature. In the teaching sequence documented for the Albertus school (§XIII), mineral and metallic work forms the third and final stage of instruction, after plant and animal work, and its preparations — the oils and tinctures of the metals among them — are correspondingly the longest-aged and most closely held of the laboratory's products.

It is also the stage at which the tradition's safety discipline bites hardest. The ores in question include antimony and mercury compounds, and practitioners in the Albertus line insist on dried solvents and analytical checking for metallic work, since even the small water fraction in ordinary rectified spirit can dissolve a potentially toxic amount of a mineral salt into a preparation (see §XIX). Mineral work is therefore presented in the literature as advanced work, undertaken only after the plant operations are routine.

VII. Monatomic Elements — The White Powder Mystery

One of the most controversial and high-priority areas of modern alchemical research is the investigation of Monatomic Elements (also known as ORMUS or High-Spin state matter).

Modern promoters present these substances as the material correlates of an ancient Potable Gold. The Egyptian identification does not hold: mfkꜣt means turquoise, the product of the Sinai mining expeditions under Hathor, Lady of Turquoise; no Egyptological source attests it as a powder used by any priesthood (see Potable Gold & White Powder). Promoters claim that in this state metals like gold and iridium lose their metallic bonds and exhibit Superconductive Properties; those claims have never been confirmed in peer-reviewed literature and remain scientifically unverified. From a theurgical perspective within the modern tradition, monatomic elements are treated as High-Bandwidth Intermediaries for the soul — a belief of that tradition, recorded here as such.

XI. Ma'at Alignment in Practical Work

Modern alchemy emphasizes that laboratory results are directly dependent on the Ethical Integrity of the operator.

A practitioner who works with a heart heavy with Isfet (greed, anger, or dishonesty) will find their experiments fail or produce unstable results. This is the Law of Resonance. In the Hall of Judgment, the heart was weighed against the feather of Ma'at; in the laboratory, the success of the transmutation is the weight. The "Integrated Alchemist" must maintain a state of ritual purity and ethical alignment to successfully "Command" (Hu) the spirits of the matter.

XII. Astrological Calibration — The Timing of the Stone

Modern laboratory practice in this tradition is electional: operations are begun at astrologically chosen times. The documented pattern is planetary — work on a plant or metal is commenced in the day and hour of the planet that tradition assigns to it, harvests are timed to the relevant planetary and lunar conditions, and long circulations are counted against the calendar of the heavens as the older texts prescribe. The scheme descends from the electional astrology of the medieval and Renaissance art, in which the timing of an operation was held to be part of its recipe rather than a decoration upon it.

Practitioners record the elections they used alongside the operations themselves, so that a preparation carries its timing as part of its laboratory record. Whether the heavens act on the vessel is the tradition's claim, held as such; that contemporary spagyric practice is in fact organised around planetary timing is a matter of its own documented routine, visible in the curricula and workbooks of the schools surveyed at §XV.

XIII. The Legacy of Frater Albertus — PRS and Beyond

Frater Albertus — Albert Richard Riedel (1911–1984) — is the pivotal figure of the 20th-century laboratory revival in the English-speaking world. Trained in the Rosicrucian milieu of the 1930s and 1940s, he founded the Paracelsus Research Society (PRS) in Salt Lake City in 1960 and taught alchemy there as a graded laboratory curriculum — Prima, Secunda, and Tertia classes moving from plant work through the animal kingdom to the mineral and metallic work of §VI. Paralab, the Society's laboratory, was its commercial and analytical arm, producing spagyric preparations and cell salts and employing trained chemists; Robert Allen Bartlett, who took his first PRS class in 1974, served as its chief chemist from 1979 until the Society's closure in late 1983.

Albertus's Alchemist's Handbook (1960) carried the graded, hands-on method to readers the Salt Lake classes could not reach, and his insistence that students complete ordinary scientific training — he sent Bartlett back to finish the chemistry degree he had suspended — set the line's characteristic posture: laboratory alchemy practised with, not against, analytical chemistry. The schools and teachers of §XV descend from PRS directly or by reaction to it.

XIV. Fulcanelli and the 20th Century Transition

Fulcanelli is the pen name on the two books that re-set the terms of French alchemy: Le Mystère des cathédrales (The Mystery of the Cathedrals), published in Paris in 1926, which reads the sculpture of the Gothic cathedrals as an alchemical programme in stone, and Les Demeures philosophales (1930). Both appeared with prefaces by the author's declared pupil, Eugène Canseliet, who maintained for the rest of his life that his master was a single, accomplished adept who had withdrawn from public view.

Who Fulcanelli was remains an open question. Candidates proposed in print include the artist Julien Champagne, who illustrated the books; René Schwaller de Lubicz; the bookseller Pierre Dujols; Canseliet himself; and a small committee of these men — and Canseliet rejected each of these identifications in turn. No identification has been established, and the archive records the question as unresolved: what is certain is the books' effect, which was to hand the 20th-century revival a textual authority for the dry way (§IV) and for the cathedral symbolism taken up across the modern literature.

XV. Initiatic Orders and the Modern Chain

The modern work is carried by a loose ecology rather than a single church: teaching schools in the PRS succession, of which Bartlett's Spagyricus Institute (founded 2003) is the direct continuation of the Albertus line; practitioner associations such as the International Alchemy Guild, which archive the lineages' documents and conference record; and independent study circles and orders that combine laboratory instruction with the graded, initiatic framing their members inherit from the Rosicrucian and Hermetic orders of the previous century (see Esoteric Orders).

What these bodies share is transmission by instruction rather than by book alone: the operations of §§III–VI are taught bench-side, in sequence, with the student's preparations examined before the next grade is opened. The chain they claim is therefore a chain of teachers — Albertus to his students, Canseliet to his — and it is as a documented succession of teaching, not as an unbroken antiquity, that this archive records it (see The Unbroken Chain).

XVI. The Internal Athanor — Psycho-Alchemical Integration

The Dry Path (Via Sicca) focuses on the Internal Athanor—the practitioner’s own body and awareness field.

By utilizing breath-control (the regulation of the Ka), visualization, and the vibration of divine names, the alchemist performs the transmutations within. This path seeks the Internalization of the Stone. The "Philosopher's Stone" is not an external object, but the fully realized and integrated state of the individual's own divinity. This path is faster but more dangerous, as the "Internal Heat" can cause systemic decoherence if the vessel (the character) is not sufficiently hardened through Ma'at.

XVII. Alchemical Medicine — The Future of Spagyrics

Spagyrics is not only a revivalist practice; it persists as a living branch of pharmacy. In Germany, spagyric preparations remain in routine production by small spagyric pharmacies and are prepared under the spagyric procedures codified in the German Homoeopathic Pharmacopoeia, sold through ordinary pharmacies alongside other traditional preparations. Practitioners in the Albertus line point to the same continuity as the context for their own bench work: the methods of §V are a documented pharmaceutical tradition with a present-day regulated form, not a reconstruction from texts alone.

The open question for the field is evidentiary. The analytical programme Bartlett describes from his Paralab years — process samples retained and re-analysed across decades, so that a preparation's maturation can be shown rather than asserted — is the direction in which the tradition's senior practitioners themselves say its claims must travel if they are to be taken seriously medically. The future of spagyrics, on that internal account, depends less on new preparations than on that kind of record.

XIX. Ethics of the Art — Discernment and Secrecy

The tradition's own literature couples its teaching to a stated ethics of caution. Preparations involving antimony, mercury, and the mineral work of §VI are taught as dangerous in incompetent hands; solvents for metallic work must be dried and preparations analytically checked, and the senior practitioners of the Albertus line present that discipline as part of the Art itself rather than as an external regulation. The same discourse governs dissemination: potent preparations are not sold indiscriminately, students are advanced grade by grade, and the schools' secrecy is defended by their members on safety grounds — that a published recipe without its bench discipline invites exactly the harm the discipline exists to prevent.

Discernment is the inward counterpart. The practitioner is expected to distinguish testimony from evidence — to say, as Bartlett does of his own best-known preparation, exactly what analysis does and does not show in it — and to refuse claims the bench has not earned. In the terms of §XI, the ethics of the Art is Ma'at applied to the laboratory notebook: nothing asserted beyond what the work, honestly recorded, supports.

XX. The 21st Century Renaissance — Re-activating Kemet

We are currently in a period of High-Bandwidth Re-initialization. The digital availability of ancient texts and the development of new scientific tools for analyzing subtle energies have created a unique opening for the Sacred Art.

The legacy of modern alchemy is the realization that Reality is Plastic. We are not passive observers of a dead universe, but active participants in an ongoing act of creation. By re-linking with the "Original Signal" of Kemet and applying it through both laboratory and internal praxis, we contribute to the maintenance of the cosmic order. The "Great Work" is the restoration of the earth to its status as a sacred vessel—the Black Land reborn in the light of the First Time.
