I. How This Bibliography Is Organised

This page is a hub utility page, and it carries no tier badge of its own: it spans all three of the hub's tiers at once, and a single badge would mislabel two-thirds of what it lists. Instead, the tier discipline is applied at the level of the sections. Every work consulted for any article in this hub appears exactly once below, in the section of the tier of the article (or articles) it grounds, with the article or articles named in a tag line under each entry.

The entries are harvested from the articles themselves — from the Sources section of each of the hub's thirty-four articles, and from §VII, "Sources and Further Reading," of the hub's index page. Nothing is listed here that no article's Sources section (or the hub's §VII) names. Where several articles rest on the same work — the hub's chronology anchor, Shaw's Oxford History of Ancient Egypt, is the standing example — the work is consolidated into a single entry and every article that uses it is tagged. Within each tier the entries run alphabetically by author or, for anonymous and institutional works, by title.

Each entry gives the citation as the article's Sources section gives it — author, title, year, and journal or publisher where the article supplies them — followed by one line on what the work grounds in this hub: the claim, date, reading, or account for which it is the cited authority. Where an article's Sources section cites a work in a deliberately limited way — "as cited in" another work, "via the discussions in" a synthesis, or as reference orientation only — this bibliography preserves that limitation rather than upgrading the citation. A reader who wants to check any claim in this hub should therefore be able to walk from the claim, to the article's Sources section, to this page, and out to the work itself without a gap.

// A NOTE ON WHAT A CITATION MEANS HERE //

A Tier A citation means the work is evidence: excavated, dated, and published material a reader can re-examine. A Tier B citation means the work is a witness: an edition or translation through which an ancient text's own claims reach us. A Tier C citation means the work is a document: a primary publication of a modern tradition, cited as the record of what that tradition teaches, or the modern scholarship that studies it. The same physical book can in principle serve in more than one of these ways; in this hub it is listed where the article that cites it stands, and the grounds line says which way it is being used.

II. Tier A — Archaeology and Science Literature

TIER A — ATTESTED

Tier A sources are the excavation reports, journal literature, and scientific studies behind the hub's twenty attested-history articles: Ochre and Symbol, Göbekli Tepe, Nabta Playa, First Metals, The Nile Bridge, Mehrgarh and the Road to the Indus, The Other Humans, Sahul, The Peopling of the Americas, Drowned Landscapes, Early Seafaring and the Obsidian Trade, The Green Sahara and Its Image-World, Jericho, Çatalhöyük, Ubaid and Uruk, The Origins of Writing, Old Europe, Megalithic Europe, Babylonian Sky Science, and Neolithic East Asia, together with the Tier A works named in the hub index's §VII. These are works whose claims can in principle be re-tested — a stratigraphy re-excavated, a date re-run, a residue re-analysed — and the articles treat them that way: where two Tier A works disagree, the disagreement is reported, not resolved by editorial preference. Dating methods, calibration, and stated uncertainty are part of the evidence, and the grounds lines below note where a work's contribution is precisely a revision — a re-dating, a re-reading of fauna, a withdrawal of an earlier interpretation — because in Tier A the corrections are as load-bearing as the discoveries.

III. Tier B — Editions and Translations of Ancient Texts

TIER B — ANCIENT TRADITION

Tier B sources are the editions, translations, and textual scholarship behind the hub's eight ancient-tradition articles: Antediluvian King Lists, Divine Dynasties, Plato's Atlantis, Flood Traditions, The Yuga Cycles, Gilgamesh — The Whole Epic, Omens and Medicine, and The Book of Enoch and the Watchers, together with the Tier B works named in the hub index's §VII. What these works ground is never the event an ancient text describes — a reign of 28,800 years, a continent sunk in a day — but the text itself: that this manuscript says this, in these words, in this translation, with these gaps. That is why the entries below name translators, editions, and even museum object presentations: in Tier B the chain of transmission is the evidence. Where an article quotes, the quotation is confined to the editions marked as quoted; everything else is paraphrase, and the grounds lines preserve that distinction. Two modern works stand inside this tier by the articles' own placement and are flagged in their grounds lines: the Black Sea deluge literature, a contested modern geological hypothesis discussed within the flood article, and Schwaller de Lubicz's Sacred Science, cited solely as the origin of a figure the Turin Canon article corrects.

IV. Tier C — Primary Esoteric Works and Modern Scholarship

TIER C — MODERN CONSTRUCTION

Tier C sources are of two kinds, and the grounds lines keep them apart. The first kind is the primary literature of the modern traditions themselves — Sclater's Lemuria paper of 1864, Blavatsky's Secret Doctrine, Churchward's Mu books, the Cayce readings as preserved by the Association for Research and Enlightenment, and the Inner Garden Foundation's published timeline — cited as publication record: author, title, and date as given, and authoritative only for what the tradition teaches. The second kind is the modern scholarship that studies those traditions — Godwin, Goodrick-Clarke, Hanegraaff, Ramaswamy, Kripal, and the archaeological profession's published responses — cited as authority for the traditions' history and reception. These are the sources behind Lemuria, Mu and Atlantis Reborn, Traditional Chronologies, Theosophical Chronology, and The Cayce Readings, and Sitchin and the Twelfth Planet, together with the Tier C works named in the hub index's §VII. A small number of scientific works appear in this section because a Tier C article cites them as the control against which a traditional chronology is checked — Bronk Ramsey et al. on dynastic radiocarbon, Hublin et al. on Jebel Irhoud, Kennedy on the Arab conquest — and their grounds lines say so. V. A Note on Consolidation, Editions, and Standing Corrections

Consolidation cuts both ways, and this page tries to be honest about both directions. A single entry above can stand behind several articles — Lambert & Millard's Atra-ḫasīs (1969) is tagged for the King Lists article, the Flood Traditions article, and the hub index, because all three rest on it — and a single article can rest on several distinct presentations of one text, which are not consolidated: Plato's Timaeus appears above in Jowett's translation, in the Lee (Penguin) version the hub index consulted, in the bilingual Elpenor presentation used to check the Greek, and in Bury's Loeb rendering of §21e, because the Atlantis article used each of them for a different check. Collapsing those into one "Plato" entry would hide exactly the information a bibliography exists to give.

Editions matter for the same reason. Where an article's Sources section records that a work was consulted indirectly — Caneva through Salvatori and Sadig, Gill through the secondary literature, Godwin's Theosophical Enlightenment through the Theosophical Society in America's review and catalogue descriptions — the entry above says so. That is not a lesser citation; it is an accurate one, and a reader following it will find the claim at the strength the article actually claimed for it.
